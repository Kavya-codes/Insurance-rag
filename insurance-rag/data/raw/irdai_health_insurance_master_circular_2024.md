# IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (2024)

## 1. Grace Period and Continuity Benefits
Under the IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (May 2024), insurers must offer a grace period for renewal of health insurance policies without loss of continuity benefits. For policies with a monthly premium payment mode, the grace period is 15 days. For policies with quarterly, half-yearly, or annual premium payment modes, the grace period is 30 days.

If the renewal premium is paid within the applicable grace period, all continuity benefits—including waiting periods already served and accrued cumulative bonus—remain intact. However, coverage is not available for the break period during which the policy remained unpaid unless the policy terms explicitly specify otherwise.

## 2. Free Look Period
Every new retail health insurance policy (other than those with a tenure of less than one year) carries a mandatory free look period of 30 days from the date of receipt of the policy document, whether received electronically or in physical form.

During the free look period, the policyholder may review the terms and conditions and request cancellation if they disagree with any clause. Where no claim has been made during the free look period, the insurer must refund the entire premium paid, deducting only expenses incurred on medical examination of the proposer and stamp duty charges, if any.

## 3. Moratorium Period
After completion of 60 continuous months (5 years) of coverage under a health insurance policy—including portability and migration across insurers—no policy and no claim can be called into question or contested by the insurer on grounds of non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except in cases of established fraud. This 60-month period is known as the Moratorium Period.

The moratorium period applies to the base sum insured and any enhanced sum insured only after 60 continuous months have elapsed from the date of such enhancement. Any claim arising after the moratorium period cannot be repudiated for undisclosed pre-existing conditions unless the insurer proves fraud.

## 4. Pre-Existing Disease (PED) and Specific Waiting Periods
A Pre-Existing Disease (PED) is defined as any condition, ailment, injury, or disease diagnosed by a physician, or for which medical advice or treatment was recommended by or received from a physician, within 36 months prior to the effective date of the policy issued by the insurer or its reinstatement.

The maximum waiting period that an insurer may impose for Pre-Existing Diseases is 36 months from the inception of the first policy. For specified slow-growing ailments and procedures (such as cataract, hernia, joint replacement, or benign prostatic hypertrophy), insurers may impose a specific waiting period not exceeding 24 months, unless the condition qualifies as a PED.

## 5. Cashless Claim Turnaround Time
Insurers must decide on requests for cashless pre-authorization within 1 hour of receipt of the request from the network hospital. Additionally, insurers must grant final authorization for discharge within 3 hours of receipt of the discharge authorization request from the hospital.

In no case shall a policyholder be made to wait at the hospital for discharge beyond 3 hours due to delay from the insurer's side. If any additional hospital charges accrue solely due to delay beyond the 3-hour final authorization window, the insurer is liable to bear such extra charges from the shareholder's fund. Insurers are encouraged to achieve 100% cashless claim settlement in a time-bound manner.

## 6. Health Insurance Portability and Migration
A policyholder covered under an indemnity health insurance policy has the right to port the policy to another general or health insurer at the time of renewal. The policyholder must submit the portability request to the new insurer at least 45 days before the expiry date of the existing policy, and not earlier than 60 days before renewal.

Upon successful portability, the new insurer must give credit for the waiting periods already served under the previous policy (including PED, specific waiting periods, and moratorium period) as well as any accrued No Claim Bonus, up to the expiring sum insured. Migration within the same insurer from one health product to another or from a group policy to an individual policy follows the same continuity credit rules.

## 7. Claim Settlement, Repudiation, and Claims Review Committee
For reimbursement claims, the insurer must settle or reject the claim within 15 days from the date of receipt of the last necessary document. In the event of delay in settlement beyond the prescribed timeline, the insurer must pay interest to the claimant at the prevailing Bank Rate plus 2% per annum from the date of receipt of the last necessary document.

No claim shall be repudiated by an insurer without formal approval from a three-member Claims Review Committee (CRC) constituted by the insurer. Claims must not be rejected on purely technical grounds such as delayed intimation if the delay was due to unavoidable circumstances and the claim is otherwise genuine.

## 8. Lifelong Renewability and AYUSH Coverage
All retail health insurance policies are guaranteed to be renewable lifelong, except on grounds of established fraud, non-disclosure, or misrepresentation by the insured. An insurer cannot refuse renewal or apply individual claim-based premium loading solely because the policyholder made a claim in the preceding policy year.

Insurers must provide coverage for AYUSH treatments (Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy) up to the full Sum Insured without any discriminatory sub-limits compared to allopathic treatments, provided the treatment is taken in a recognized or accredited AYUSH hospital or day-care centre.
